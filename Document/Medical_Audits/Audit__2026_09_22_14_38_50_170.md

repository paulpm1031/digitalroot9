# Operational Bottleneck Analysis: The Rosewood Clinic

**Scope:** Booking conversion path, pre-consultation administrative workflow, and post-consultation communication loops.
**Method:** Lean Six Sigma waste analysis (motion, waiting, over-processing, defects) applied to scraped front-end and review data.

---

## 1. Key Findings

### Booking Friction: **CONFIRMED — SEVERE (Highest-Impact Bottleneck)**

The booking flow contains a **fatal conversion defect on the highest-intent page on the website**. The "Make an appointment" section — the exact moment a patient has decided to book — surfaces a **Cloudflare Turnstile widget directly in the booking path**, with visible failure states scraped from the live page:

> *"Checking your Browser… Verifying…"*
> *"Verification failed"*
> *"Verification expired — [Refresh]"*
> *"Stuck? [Troubleshoot]"*

This is not a theoretical risk. The page is serving **expired and failed challenge states to real visitors**, and the only recovery affordance is a "Refresh"/"Troubleshoot" link that dumps the patient onto a Cloudflare help page. Every patient who hits "Verification expired" at this moment either refreshes, abandons, or falls back to the phone — and the scrape shows **no real-time slot calendar, no instant confirmation, and no digital booking alternative** rendered on the page.

Secondary friction: the only persistent conversion channels are a **landline (03 4810 6037)** and a **manual WhatsApp line (013 575 8121)**. Both are human-gated. A prospect researching at 9pm on a Sunday (clinic is closed Sunday — see below) cannot book. They can only *hope* someone replies to a WhatsApp message the next business day. In a functional medicine market where competitors offer instant online scheduling, this is a leak at the top of the funnel.

**Estimated leakage mechanism:** High-intent visitor → hits Turnstile failure → no self-serve fallback → abandons → books with a competitor.

---

### Administrative Gaps: **CONFIRMED — MODERATE-TO-SEVERE**

The clinic's own published 4-step process reveals the gap:

> **01 Data Gathering** — "a detailed patient intake questionnaire, biometric measurements, and a review (if applicable) of past patient medical records, and blood tests."
>
> **02 Consultation & Tests** — "The 1+ hour consultation with our doctor…"

There is **no evidence anywhere in the scraped data of a digital intake link being sent post-booking**. The intake questionnaire is described as a step in the process, not as something delivered electronically the moment a booking is made. Combined with the manual booking channels (phone/WhatsApp), the operational reality is almost certainly:

1. Patient books via phone/WhatsApp (manual, staff time).
2. Staff manually re-enter patient details into a system.
3. Patient arrives and completes intake on paper or via a PDF emailed after the fact.
4. Doctor spends the first 10–15 minutes of a **1+ hour consultation** re-validating or chasing intake data.

This is classic **duplicated data entry + pre-consultation dead time**. It burns front-desk capacity, erodes the doctor's billable hour, and delays the actual clinical work. For a clinic whose differentiator is *depth* (genetic testing, metabolic panels, comprehensive bloodwork), losing consultation minutes to admin is a direct hit to the product itself.

---

### Communication Failures: **CONFIRMED — SEVERE (Structural, Not Just Behavioral)**

Two hard structural defects are visible in the scrape:

**1. Sunday closure with zero after-hours capture.**
> *"Sunday: CLOSED"*

The clinic is dark every Sunday. All booking channels (landline, WhatsApp, web form behind a broken Turnstile) are human-gated. A patient who decides to book on a Sunday — a peak research window for health decisions — hits a wall. There is **no automated after-hours response, no instant booking confirmation, and no lead capture** shown. Those leads are simply dropped.

**2. No structured retention loop despite a review base that proves demand.**

The reviews (Amelia Chen, Jeffrey Wright, Priti Wright, sarahbasir, etc.) are uniformly positive about the *clinical* experience — Dr Deanna, Dr Krish, Dr Iman, Husna, and the staff are repeatedly praised. But look at what the reviews *don't* contain: any mention of reminders, follow-up check-ins, or a structured review/evaluation loop. The clinic's own Step 04 is "Evaluation & Review — We monitor, review, and optimise" — yet nothing in the scraped operational data shows an automated trigger for that step. Retention depends entirely on the patient remembering to rebook. For a functional medicine model built on longitudinal metabolic and hormonal tracking, **the absence of a post-consultation retention sequence is a revenue leak, not an inconvenience**.

Compounding factor: the 1+ hour consultation model means **slots are scarce and high-value**. A no-show or a forgotten follow-up is disproportionately expensive compared to a standard GP clinic.
